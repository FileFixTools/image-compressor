How Mortgage Payments Work

A mortgage payment is calculated by amortizing your loan amount over the loan term at a fixed interest rate, so that each monthly payment is the same size, but the mix between principal and interest changes over time. Early in the loan, most of each payment goes toward interest; later in the loan, most of it goes toward paying down the principal balance.

What Is PITI?

PITI stands for Principal, Interest, Taxes, and Insurance — the four components most lenders bundle into a single monthly housing payment:

  • Principal: The portion of your payment that reduces your loan balance.
  • Interest: The cost of borrowing, calculated on your remaining balance.
  • Taxes: Property taxes, usually collected monthly and held in escrow.
  • Insurance: Homeowners insurance, and PMI if your down payment is under 20%.

What Is PMI and When Do You Need It?

Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI) protects the lender if you default on a conventional loan with a down payment below 20%. It's typically charged as a percentage of the loan amount per year, split into monthly installments, and can usually be removed once you reach 20% equity in your home.

Why Extra Payments Save Money

Adding extra money to your monthly payment goes directly toward your principal balance, which reduces the amount of interest that accrues on future payments. Even modest extra payments can shorten your loan term by years and save a significant amount in total interest, which this calculator shows automatically when you enter an extra monthly payment amount.
