Understanding Your Loan Payments

Whether you're financing a car, consolidating debt, or taking out a personal loan, knowing your exact monthly payment — and how much of it goes toward interest versus principal — helps you plan your budget with confidence. This free loan calculator uses the same standard amortization formula lenders use, so you get an accurate, realistic estimate before you ever sign a loan agreement.

Simply enter your loan amount, interest rate, and term, and the calculator instantly works out your monthly payment, total interest paid over the life of the loan, and a full amortization schedule. You can also model extra monthly payments to see how much faster you could become debt-free and how much interest you'd save along the way.

Principal vs. Interest: What's the Difference?

Every loan payment is split into two parts. The principal is the portion that goes toward paying down the original amount you borrowed. The interest is the cost of borrowing that money, calculated as a percentage of your remaining balance. Early in a loan, a larger share of each payment goes toward interest; as the balance shrinks, more of each payment goes toward principal — this pattern is called amortization.

How Loan Payments Work

Most installment loans use a fixed monthly payment calculated so that the loan is fully paid off — principal and interest — by the end of the term. Each month, interest is charged on your current outstanding balance, and whatever is left of your payment reduces the principal. Because the balance decreases every month, the interest portion decreases too, while the principal portion increases, even though your total payment stays the same.

Why Extra Payments Make Such a Big Difference

Because interest is calculated on your remaining balance, any extra amount you put toward principal reduces the balance that future interest is calculated on — for every remaining month of the loan. That's why even a modest extra monthly payment can shave months or years off your payoff time and save a meaningful amount in total interest.
